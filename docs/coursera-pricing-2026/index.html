The pricing news you probably haven't heard: prices went DOWN, not up

Here's what we found digging into "coursera price increase" and "coursera price 2026": we could not verify a 2025 or 2026 price increase for US customers. What we did find is that Coursera launched region-aligned pricing in September 2025, cutting prices by up to 60% across 100+ countries. In India, for example, Specializations and Professional Certificates now start around ₹1,699/month and Coursera Plus annual runs about ₹13,999 — a fraction of the US dollar-equivalent price. The Philippines got a similar rollout.

If you're in the US or another full-price market, this doesn't put more money in your pocket directly — but it tells you something useful: Coursera's 2025–2026 pricing strategy has been about expanding access at lower price points elsewhere, not squeezing existing customers harder. If you were bracing for a price hike, the evidence doesn't support one.

One structural change worth knowing about, even though it doesn't touch your bill directly: in 2025–2026, Coursera began charging content partners (the universities and companies whose courses you're taking) a platform fee, reportedly around 15%. Learner-facing prices weren't affected, but at least one university has reportedly pulled content over it. The practical implication for you as a subscriber: the catalog you're paying for isn't necessarily fixed — course availability can shift as partner economics change. Worth knowing if you're subscribing to Plus specifically for one university's content.

Coursera Plus: the actual math

At roughly $59/month or $399/year (about $33/month if you pay annually), Plus gets you unlimited access to most — not all — of the catalog. Annual pricing drops well below list during Coursera's frequent promotions (Black Friday and New Year sales commonly bring the annual plan under $250), so check for a current discount before paying list price.

What's reliably excluded from Plus: degrees and MasterTrack Certificates. What's murkier: some premium partner content and — this genuinely varies by certificate and changes over time — certain Professional Certificates. The only way to know for sure: open the specific course or certificate you want and look for the "Included with Coursera Plus" badge. Don't assume; check.

Buying a single course or certificate instead

If you only want one thing, paying separately is often cheaper than a Plus subscription — but the buying model isn't uniform across Coursera's catalog. Some individual courses sell as one-time purchases; others (especially Specializations and Professional Certificates) bill monthly until you finish, which quietly punishes anyone who takes longer than planned. Check which model applies before you commit — it changes your real cost more than the sticker price does.

Professional Certificates (the Google, IBM, Meta, and Microsoft career-track programs) commonly run in the $39–99/month range, and most people take considerably longer than Coursera's advertised timeline — see our guide to Coursera's career certificates for the honest completion-time reality and whether these programs are worth it.

Financial aid: how it actually works

Coursera offers financial aid on individual courses (not always Specializations) through a short application — typically two ~150-word essays about your circumstances and goals. Decisions reportedly take about two weeks, aid covers the full course cost, and it's valid for a set window (commonly cited around 180 days) before you'd need to reapply. One thing we won't do: state an approval rate. Several sites claim financial aid is "almost always approved," but we couldn't find Coursera-published data backing that up, and repeating an unverified number as fact wouldn't be honest. Apply and see — the downside of trying is only the time it takes to write two paragraphs.

The refund policy, in plain terms

  • One-time purchases and annual Coursera Plus: refundable within 14 days, but the window closes early if you've already earned a certificate on that purchase
  • Monthly subscriptions (including monthly Plus): cancel to stop future billing — there's generally no refund on the current billing period
  • Guided Projects: typically non-refundable
  • Promotional pricing: renews at full list price the following term unless you cancel first — this is the single most common billing complaint about subscription services generally, and Coursera is no exception. Set a calendar reminder.

Frequently asked questions

Did Coursera actually raise prices in 2026? We couldn't verify a consumer-facing price increase. The documented 2025–2026 change runs the other direction — expanded regional pricing that lowered costs in many countries. If you saw a higher price, check whether you're being shown a different plan/region default than you expect, or whether a promotional rate you had simply expired.

Is Coursera Plus worth it if I only want one certificate? Usually no — see the full Plus review for the break-even math. Roughly speaking, Plus starts winning once you're completing two or more Specializations/certificates in a year.

Does financial aid really work? It exists, it's real, and it's worth applying for if cost is the barrier — just don't take a specific "approval rate" you read online at face value, since Coursera doesn't publish one.

What's not included in Coursera Plus? Degrees and MasterTrack Certificates, reliably. Beyond that, check the badge on the specific course — inclusion isn't uniform across the whole catalog.
